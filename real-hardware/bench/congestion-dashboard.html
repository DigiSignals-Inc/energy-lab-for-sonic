The story — normal traffic, then congestion that heals itself
Deep dive — the two extremes
● Measured on real metal. Every number is read live from two physical Dell Z9100 switches (community SONiC) and two ConnectX hosts over SSH/gNMI. The 25G egress is a real breakout port (QSFP→4×SFP28). Healthy baseline = a steady 12G RoCE flow with headroom. Inject congestion adds an aggressor so demand exceeds 25G — DCQCN throttles the senders via PFC + ECN and holds it lossless (0 drops). Lossy flood = plain UDP, so the switch just drops the overflow (no feedback). Single-flow loop = one RoCE queue pair, showing the control loop without many flows averaging it out. Congestion control here is ConnectX firmware DCQCN, not the vanilla Zhu et al. (SIGCOMM 2015) algorithm — the sender-side state machine differs in fast recovery and target-rate clamping. ECN now reads from the standard SAI counter (SAI_QUEUE_STAT_WRED_ECN_MARKED_PACKETS) over gNMI, after enabling the WRED_ECN_QUEUE_STAT group that SONiC ships disabled.
